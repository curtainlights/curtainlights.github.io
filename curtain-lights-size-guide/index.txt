2d:T22cd,<h2 id="how-to-measure-in-five-minutes">How to measure in five minutes</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Choose the mounting line.</strong> Decide where the top cable will hang: the top of the frame, a curtain rod, ceiling hooks or the underside of the balcony above.</li>
<li><strong>Measure the width</strong> along that line, not across the glass. If the hooks sit outside the frame, measure hook to hook.</li>
<li><strong>Measure the drop</strong> from the mounting line to where the strands should end: the sill, the floor or the top of a glass railing. Leave about 2 in (5 cm) so strands don&#39;t drag.</li>
<li><strong>Note obstacles:</strong> handles, hinges, blinds, a door that swings into the space.</li>
<li><strong>Find the power.</strong> Measure the distance from the curtain&#39;s top corner to the nearest outlet. Lumalyn doesn&#39;t list its USB cable length, so if the outlet is far away, plan for a power bank or a USB extension cable kept indoors or dry.</li>
<li><strong>Write it down in both units</strong>, since sets are often sold in meters.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="led-density-the-number-most-size-charts-skip">LED density: the number most size charts skip</h2>

<p>Two points trip people up. First, a railing is far shorter than any curtain&#39;s drop, so clipping a 6 ft 7 in curtain to the rail leaves a pile of strands at the bottom. Hanging it from the ceiling or the balcony above lets it fall straight to the floor, which looks better and shows the full design through glass railings.</p>
<p>Second, a curtain wider than a small window isn&#39;t a problem if you mount it on a rod that runs past the frame or spans a pair of windows. For window-specific tips, see our <a href="/window-curtain-lights/">window curtain lights guide</a>.</p>
<h2 id="3m-x-3m-curtain-lights-when-the-big-size-makes-sense">3m x 3m curtain lights: when the big size makes sense</h2>
<p>A 3m x 3m curtain is impressive, but it&#39;s not the default choice. It&#39;s 9 ft 10 in tall, so in a room or balcony with an 8 to 9 ft ceiling, the bottom 1–2 ft will rest on the floor. On a plain curtain that&#39;s fine. On a pixel curtain, the bunched rows hide part of every picture.</p>
<p>Choose 3 × 3 m when you have:</p>
<ul>
<li>double-height windows, lofts or ceilings of about 10 ft (3 m) or more;</li>
<li>a wide sliding door or glass wall where coverage matters more than crisp detail;</li>
<li>an event backdrop hung from a beam or stand;</li>
<li>an audience that mostly sees it from a distance, like a street below.</li>
</ul>
<p>Otherwise, the 2 × 2 m set fits more homes and gives the sharper image.</p>

<h2 id="when-the-size-isn-39-t-exact">When the size isn&#39;t exact</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Slightly too wide:</strong> use a rod that extends past the frame, or center the curtain and let the edges overlap the wall.</li>
<li><strong>Slightly too long:</strong> raise the mounting line, or let the bottom rest on the sill or floor. Looping extra length over the rod works for plain curtains but hides rows on a pixel curtain.</li>
<li><strong>Too short:</strong> mount it higher, from ceiling hooks or the balcony above.</li>
<li><strong>Windows that open:</strong> keep handles and hinges clear so you can still use them.</li>
</ul>
