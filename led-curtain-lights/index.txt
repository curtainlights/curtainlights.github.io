25:T2200,<h2 id="what-led-curtain-lights-are">What LED curtain lights are</h2>
<p>An LED curtain is a row of thin light strands hanging from one top cable, a bit like a bead curtain made of tiny bulbs. You hang the top line from hooks, a rod or a frame, and the strands drop down to fill a rectangle with light.</p>
<p>Because it covers an area rather than a line, a curtain fills a window or a glass balcony in a way a single string never can. It also reads well from a distance. That&#39;s why curtains are a favorite for street-facing windows, balcony glass, party backdrops and bedroom walls.</p>
<p>Modern sets fall into two families, and picking between them is the biggest decision you&#39;ll make.</p>

<p>A <strong>plain fairy curtain</strong> is lovely behind sheer drapes or above a bed. Many sets offer a handful of preset modes, and that&#39;s the whole menu.</p>
<p>A <strong>pixel curtain</strong> treats every LED as a dot on a large, soft screen. That&#39;s what lets it draw a tree, a heart or a name. It costs more than a basic string, but one curtain can carry you through Christmas, a birthday, game night and Halloween instead of sitting in a box eleven months a year.</p>
<p>If you only want a warm glow, start with our <a href="/curtain-fairy-lights/">curtain fairy lights guide</a>. If you want a window that changes with the calendar, a pixel curtain is the better buy.</p>
<h2 id="size-and-led-count-why-density-matters">Size and LED count: why density matters</h2>
<p>Curtains are sold by width × drop. You&#39;ll often see sizes from about 3 ft 3 in (1 m) square up to 9 ft 10 in (3 m) square, with the total LED count listed beside the size.</p>
<p>For a plain curtain, more LEDs mostly means more glow. For a pixel curtain, the number to watch is <strong>density</strong>: how many LEDs share each square foot. Pictures and letters look crisper when the dots sit closer together.</p>

<h2 id="power-plug-in-usb-or-battery">Power: plug-in, USB or battery</h2>
<p>Curtain lights run on low voltage, but they get it in different ways:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mains adapter.</strong> Many plain curtains come with a plug-in transformer. Simple, but you need an outlet close by.</li>
<li><strong>USB.</strong> A growing number of sets, Lumalyn&#39;s included, take 5 V USB. Any phone charger works, and so does a power bank, which is handy on a balcony with no outlet.</li>
<li><strong>Batteries.</strong> Some small curtains run on AA cells. Fine for one party, tiresome for nightly use.</li>
</ul>
<p>One thing to know up front: Lumalyn does <strong>not</strong> include a USB adapter in the box. Most homes have a spare phone charger in a drawer, but check before the curtain arrives so the first evening isn&#39;t spent hunting for one. The remote also needs AAA batteries, which aren&#39;t included.</p>

<h2 id="controls-remote-app-music-and-timer">Controls: remote, app, music and timer</h2>
<p>How you control a curtain decides whether you&#39;ll still enjoy it in week three.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Remote:</strong> fast on/off and mode changes from the sofa.</li>
<li><strong>App:</strong> browse scenes, pick colors, set brightness and speed.</li>
<li><strong>Music sync:</strong> the lights move with the music in the room.</li>
<li><strong>Timer:</strong> the curtain switches on and off by itself.</li>
</ul>
<p>Lumalyn pairs a Bluetooth app for iOS and Android with a remote. No Wi-Fi network is needed. The trade-off is that Bluetooth works from nearby, so you control it while you&#39;re home rather than from across town. For a window or balcony, that&#39;s usually when you&#39;re enjoying it anyway. The timer handles the rest. Our <a href="/smart-curtain-lights/">smart curtain lights guide</a> compares Bluetooth and Wi-Fi control.</p>
