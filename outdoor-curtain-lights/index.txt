24:T2003,<h2 id="covered-or-exposed-decide-where-the-curtain-will-live">Covered or exposed? Decide where the curtain will live</h2>
<p>With LED curtain lights outdoors, the two things that cause trouble are water and power, and both start with the spot. Before you look at any product, look at where it will hang.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Spot</th>
<th>Exposure</th>
<th>What to look for</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Inside a street-facing window</td>
<td>None</td>
<td>Any indoor-rated curtain</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Balcony under the floor above</td>
<td>Sheltered, with some wind-blown splashes</td>
<td>IP44 or higher</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Covered porch or veranda</td>
<td>Sheltered</td>
<td>IP44 or higher</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Pergola with open slats</td>
<td>Partly exposed</td>
<td>IP65 or higher</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Open railing, fence, tree or roof edge</td>
<td>Full rain and snow</td>
<td>IP65 or higher</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Ground level where puddles form</td>
<td>Possible pooling</td>
<td>IP67</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>A simple test: after the next rain, check the spot. If the floor where the curtain would hang stays dry, it&#39;s sheltered. If it&#39;s soaked, treat it as exposed.</p>
<p>Remember the window option, too. Hang a curtain on the inside of a street-facing window and the whole display shows outside with no weather to worry about. Our <a href="/window-curtain-lights/">window curtain lights guide</a> covers glare, blinds and condensation.</p>
<h2 id="ip-ratings-in-plain-english">IP ratings in plain English</h2>
<p>An IP code is &quot;IP&quot; plus two digits. The first rates protection against solids such as dust; the second rates protection against water.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Rating</th>
<th>Solids</th>
<th>Water</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>IP44</td>
<td>Objects over 1 mm, like wires</td>
<td>Splashing from any direction</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>IP65</td>
<td>Dust-tight</td>
<td>Water jets from any direction</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>IP67</td>
<td>Dust-tight</td>
<td>Temporary immersion, up to 1 m (3 ft 3 in)</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>UK charity Electrical Safety First says lights suitable for outdoor use should carry an IP water rating of three or higher. IP44 clears that bar for splashes, which is why it suits covered spots. For lights that face open rain, a jet-rated IP65 set is the safer choice.</p>
<p>One detail many shoppers miss: check that the rating covers the controller and plug, not just the strands. Lumalyn is clear about this. The curtain is IP44, and the USB plug must stay dry.</p>
<h2 id="safe-outdoor-power-gfci-rcd-and-dry-plugs">Safe outdoor power: GFCI, RCD and dry plugs</h2>
<p>Light strands are only half the setup. The power side deserves the same care.</p>
<p>In the U.S., the Consumer Product Safety Commission advises checking outdoor lights for labels certifying them for outdoor use, and plugging them only into a GFCI-protected outlet or a portable GFCI. It also recommends checking every set, new or old, for cracked sockets, frayed or bare wires and loose connections, and making sure any extension cord is rated for the job.</p>
<p>In the UK, Electrical Safety First recommends a 30 mA RCD-protected socket for lights plugged in outdoors. For outdoor leads, it advises weather-resistant cables with moulded connections and fully unwinding cable reels, which can overheat when left coiled.</p>
<p>USB-powered curtains make this easier. The mains part, a phone charger, can stay indoors while only the low-voltage USB lead runs outside. A power bank in a dry corner of the balcony also works. Either way:</p>
<ul>
<li>keep the charger, USB plug and controller out of rain and splashes;</li>
<li>don&#39;t pinch the cable in a closing door or window;</li>
<li>don&#39;t run the cable where people will trip over it.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="wind-railings-and-fixing-points">Wind, railings and fixing points</h2>
<p>Wind can tangle the strands and swing them against a railing. Secure both ends:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Top line:</strong> hang it from hooks or a rod at several points, not just the two corners.</li>
<li><strong>Bottom corners:</strong> clip or tie them to the frame, the railing or small floor hooks so the strands can&#39;t flap.</li>
<li><strong>Strands:</strong> keep them straight and evenly spaced. On a pixel curtain, that spacing is what keeps pictures and text readable.</li>
<li><strong>No-drill options:</strong> renters can use adhesive hooks, tension rods or railing clips. Check your building&#39;s rules on balcony decorations first.</li>
</ul>
<p>Avoid sharp metal edges where cables could rub through, and never staple or nail through a wire.</p>
<h2 id="brightness-neighbors-and-timers">Brightness, neighbors and timers</h2>
<p>A display people love beats one they complain about. Set the brightness for the viewing distance: a balcony seen from across the street rarely needs full power. Slower scenes also feel calmer than rapid flashing.</p>
<p>A timer keeps the show on schedule, on at dusk and off before neighbors sleep, without you remembering every night. Electrical Safety First also advises switching lights off and unplugging them before bed or when you go out, so plan a nightly off time.</p>
<p>Lumalyn&#39;s curtain includes a timer plus adjustable brightness and speed.</p>
<h2 id="taking-it-down-and-storing-it">Taking it down and storing it</h2>
<p>How you store the curtain decides whether it works next season.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Dry it completely.</strong> Electrical Safety First warns against using electrical equipment outdoors even when there&#39;s just dew on the ground, and the same thinking applies to packing. Bring the curtain in and let it dry before it goes in a box.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the strands parallel.</strong> Lay it flat and roll it from the bottom up, or wrap it around a stiff piece of cardboard.</li>
<li><strong>Store controller and cables together.</strong> Use the original box or a labeled bag.</li>
<li><strong>Take the batteries out of the remote</strong> before long storage.</li>
<li><strong>Inspect before reuse.</strong> Look for damaged cables and loose connections before you hang it again.</li>
</ol>
