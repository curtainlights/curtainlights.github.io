Covered or exposed? Decide where the curtain will live

With LED curtain lights outdoors, the two things that cause trouble are water and power, and both start with the spot. Before you look at any product, look at where it will hang.

Spot Exposure What to look for
Inside a street-facing window None Any indoor-rated curtain
Balcony under the floor above Sheltered, with some wind-blown splashes IP44 or higher
Covered porch or veranda Sheltered IP44 or higher
Pergola with open slats Partly exposed IP65 or higher
Open railing, fence, tree or roof edge Full rain and snow IP65 or higher
Ground level where puddles form Possible pooling IP67

A simple test: after the next rain, check the spot. If the floor where the curtain would hang stays dry, it's sheltered. If it's soaked, treat it as exposed.

Remember the window option, too. Hang a curtain on the inside of a street-facing window and the whole display shows outside with no weather to worry about. Our window curtain lights guide covers glare, blinds and condensation.

IP ratings in plain English

An IP code is "IP" plus two digits. The first rates protection against solids such as dust; the second rates protection against water.

Rating Solids Water
IP44 Objects over 1 mm, like wires Splashing from any direction
IP65 Dust-tight Water jets from any direction
IP67 Dust-tight Temporary immersion, up to 1 m (3 ft 3 in)

UK charity Electrical Safety First says lights suitable for outdoor use should carry an IP water rating of three or higher. IP44 clears that bar for splashes, which is why it suits covered spots. For lights that face open rain, a jet-rated IP65 set is the safer choice.

One detail many shoppers miss: check that the rating covers the controller and plug, not just the strands. Lumalyn is clear about this. The curtain is IP44, and the USB plug must stay dry.

Safe outdoor power: GFCI, RCD and dry plugs

Light strands are only half the setup. The power side deserves the same care.

In the U.S., the Consumer Product Safety Commission advises checking outdoor lights for labels certifying them for outdoor use, and plugging them only into a GFCI-protected outlet or a portable GFCI. It also recommends checking every set, new or old, for cracked sockets, frayed or bare wires and loose connections, and making sure any extension cord is rated for the job.

In the UK, Electrical Safety First recommends a 30 mA RCD-protected socket for lights plugged in outdoors. For outdoor leads, it advises weather-resistant cables with moulded connections and fully unwinding cable reels, which can overheat when left coiled.

USB-powered curtains make this easier. The mains part, a phone charger, can stay indoors while only the low-voltage USB lead runs outside. A power bank in a dry corner of the balcony also works. Either way:

  • keep the charger, USB plug and controller out of rain and splashes;
  • don't pinch the cable in a closing door or window;
  • don't run the cable where people will trip over it.

Wind, railings and fixing points

Wind can tangle the strands and swing them against a railing. Secure both ends:

  • Top line: hang it from hooks or a rod at several points, not just the two corners.
  • Bottom corners: clip or tie them to the frame, the railing or small floor hooks so the strands can't flap.
  • Strands: keep them straight and evenly spaced. On a pixel curtain, that spacing is what keeps pictures and text readable.
  • No-drill options: renters can use adhesive hooks, tension rods or railing clips. Check your building's rules on balcony decorations first.

Avoid sharp metal edges where cables could rub through, and never staple or nail through a wire.

Brightness, neighbors and timers

A display people love beats one they complain about. Set the brightness for the viewing distance: a balcony seen from across the street rarely needs full power. Slower scenes also feel calmer than rapid flashing.

A timer keeps the show on schedule, on at dusk and off before neighbors sleep, without you remembering every night. Electrical Safety First also advises switching lights off and unplugging them before bed or when you go out, so plan a nightly off time.

Taking it down and storing it

How you store the curtain decides whether it works next season.

  1. Dry it completely. Electrical Safety First warns against using electrical equipment outdoors even when there's just dew on the ground, and the same thinking applies to packing. Bring the curtain in and let it dry before it goes in a box.
  2. Keep the strands parallel. Lay it flat and roll it from the bottom up, or wrap it around a stiff piece of cardboard.
  3. Store controller and cables together. Use the original box or a labeled bag.
  4. Take the batteries out of the remote before long storage.
  5. Inspect before reuse. Look for damaged cables and loose connections before you hang it again.

Frequently asked questions

Can I leave outdoor curtain lights out in the rain?

Only if the whole set is rated for it. Spots open to rain need IP65 or higher on the lights, the controller and the plug. IP44 sets like Lumalyn's are made for sheltered outdoor spots, such as a covered balcony, and shouldn't sit in open rain.

What IP rating do outdoor curtain lights need?

IP44 handles water splashed from any direction, which suits covered balconies and porches. IP65 resists water jets, which suits exposed spots. IP67 adds protection against temporary immersion for low spots where water can pool.

Do outdoor curtain lights need a GFCI outlet?

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission advises plugging outdoor lights only into a GFCI-protected outlet or a portable GFCI. In the UK, Electrical Safety First says outdoor lights should run from a 30 mA RCD-protected socket.

Can LED curtain lights outdoors run on a power bank?

USB-powered curtains can. Lumalyn's 5 V USB curtain works with any USB charger or power bank, which helps on balconies without an outlet. Keep the power bank and the USB plug dry.

How do I stop curtain lights blowing in the wind?

Fix the top line at several points, then clip or tie the bottom corners to the frame, railing or floor hooks. On a pixel curtain, straight, evenly spaced strands also keep pictures and text readable.
