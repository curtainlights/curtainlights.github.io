2c:T1e72,<h2 id="what-makes-curtain-lights-smart">What makes curtain lights smart</h2>
<p>Smart curtain lights typically fall into three tiers, and the tier matters more than the word &quot;smart&quot; on the box.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Tier</th>
<th>How you control it</th>
<th>What it can do</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Basic</td>
<td>A button on the cable or a simple remote</td>
<td>On, off and a few preset modes</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>App-controlled color</td>
<td>An app changes the whole curtain or whole strands</td>
<td>Colors, brightness, presets, often a timer</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Pixel (addressable)</td>
<td>An app sets every LED individually</td>
<td>Pictures, scrolling text, animations, your own drawings</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>The jump from the second tier to the third is the big one. Once each LED can be set on its own, the curtain works like a low-resolution display. The Lumalyn set sits in that third tier with 400 individually controlled LEDs. For a broader look at curtain types, see /led-curtain-lights/.</p>
<h2 id="bluetooth-vs-wi-fi-the-real-trade-off">Bluetooth vs. Wi-Fi: the real trade-off</h2>
<p>App controlled curtain lights reach your phone in one of two ways, and each has a catch.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th></th>
<th>Bluetooth</th>
<th>Wi-Fi</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Setup</td>
<td>Pair your phone directly with the lights</td>
<td>Join the lights to your home network, typically through an account</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Where you can control it</td>
<td>Nearby: typically up to about 33 ft (10 m), less through walls</td>
<td>Anywhere with an internet connection</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>If your internet goes down</td>
<td>Still works</td>
<td>Depends on the set</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Voice assistants</td>
<td>Rarely</td>
<td>Commonly supported</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Adds a device to your network</td>
<td>No</td>
<td>Yes</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>Lumalyn&#39;s curtain uses Bluetooth with an app for iOS and Android, plus a physical remote, and needs no Wi-Fi. On paper that&#39;s the simpler setup, with nothing joining your home network, and for a living room, bedroom or balcony you&#39;re standing next to, range is rarely a problem. The trade-off is real, though: you can&#39;t switch it on from the office, and Lumalyn doesn&#39;t list Alexa or Google Home support. If voice control matters to you, a Wi-Fi set is the better fit.</p>
<h2 id="pixel-effects-what-400-individually-controlled-leds-buy-you">Pixel effects: what 400 individually controlled LEDs buy you</h2>
<p>Each LED on a pixel curtain can show its own color at its own moment, which is what makes moving pictures possible. Lumalyn lists 100+ animated scenes across Christmas, snow and winter, festive messages, color patterns, other holiday themes and ambient lighting, with 16 million colors and adjustable brightness and speed.</p>
<p>Size changes how sharp those pictures look. Both Lumalyn sizes have 400 LEDs:</p>

<p>If you mostly want images and words, the 2 m set is the smarter pick. Our /curtain-lights-size-guide/ goes deeper on measuring.</p>
<h2 id="music-sync-diy-drawing-and-scrolling-text">Music sync, DIY drawing and scrolling text</h2>
<p>These are the features that make people pull out their phones to film your window.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Music sync.</strong> Lumalyn lists five music-sync modes that make the display move with whatever&#39;s playing. It&#39;s the party trick: put on a playlist and the curtain becomes part of the room.</li>
<li><strong>DIY drawing.</strong> Draw your own pixel designs in the app: a heart, your team&#39;s colors, a birthday cake, or a pumpkin for /halloween-curtain-lights/. Simple, bold shapes work best on a grid.</li>
<li><strong>Scrolling text.</strong> Type a message and it scrolls across the curtain: &quot;Happy birthday Mia&quot;, &quot;Welcome home&quot;, &quot;Go team&quot;. One tip: a light curtain is visible from both sides, but text reads correctly from only one. Lumalyn doesn&#39;t list a mirror setting, so check your message from where people will see it.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="timers-and-the-remote-the-features-you-39-ll-use-daily">Timers and the remote: the features you&#39;ll use daily</h2>
<p>The flashy features get the attention, but the plain ones decide whether you still love the lights a month later. Lumalyn lists a timer that turns the curtain on and off automatically, which covers the main reason people want remote access in the first place: a display that&#39;s on every evening without anyone touching it.</p>
<p>The remote matters too. Guests, kids and anyone not in the mood to open an app can change scenes or switch off with a button. It needs AAA batteries, which aren&#39;t included. In a bedroom, the remote also lets you dim the lights without staring at a bright phone screen; see /curtain-lights-for-bedroom/.</p>
<h2 id="who-actually-needs-smart-curtain-lights">Who actually needs smart curtain lights?</h2>
<p><strong>Smart is worth it if you:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>want pictures, text or animations, not just a glow;</li>
<li>like changing the look for holidays, birthdays and game nights;</li>
<li>host parties and want music sync;</li>
<li>want a timer so the display runs itself.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>You can skip smart if you:</strong></p>
<ul>

<p>Smart only pays off when the LEDs are addressable, and that&#39;s exactly where the Lumalyn curtain sits: 400 pixels, 100+ scenes, five music modes, DIY drawing, scrolling text, a timer and a remote, with no Wi-Fi setup to wrestle with. Choose it if you&#39;ll be near the lights when you use them, which describes most windows, living rooms and balconies. Skip it if voice control or away-from-home control is a must.</p>
