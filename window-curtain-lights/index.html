Inside or outside the glass?

For most homes, inside wins.

The weather row decides it. Lumalyn rates its curtain IP44. In the IP code, that second 4 means protection against water splashing from any direction, a step below the water-jet protection of IPX5 or IP65. So the set is fine indoors and on sheltered balconies, but it isn't made for open rain, and the USB plug must stay dry. If you're set on lighting the outside, read /outdoor-curtain-lights/ first.

Glare and reflections: making the glass work for you

At night, a lit room turns a window into a mirror, and a curtain of LEDs can show up twice: once for real and once reflected. A few habits fix most of it:

  • Hang close to the glass. The nearer the curtain is to the pane, the closer its reflection sits to the real LEDs, so the doubling is much less noticeable.
  • Dim the room. Turn off overhead lights so people outside see the curtain rather than your living room, and you see less reflection from inside.
  • Lower the brightness for indoor viewing. A pixel curtain at full brightness a few feet from the sofa can be dazzling. Lumalyn lists adjustable brightness, so turn it down in the evening.
  • Test text from outside. A light curtain is visible from both sides, but text reads correctly from only one. Lumalyn doesn't list a mirror setting, so scroll a test word and look from the sidewalk before you rely on messages. Symmetrical pictures look fine either way.

Double-glazed windows can show a faint second reflection. That's ordinary glass behavior, not a fault in the lights.

Height is the other half. Each curtain is as tall as it is wide, so on a window shorter than the curtain, the strands will run past the sill. Let them hang toward the floor if you can. Working with blinds and drapes

  • Between the glass and blinds. Mount the curtain on a tension rod inside the window recess or on hooks along the top of the frame, then lower the blinds behind it. From outside, the closed blinds become a clean backdrop; inside, they block the glow at bedtime. Check that the blinds still move freely.
  • Behind sheer curtains. Hang the lights against the glass with the sheers in front. Inside you get a soft, diffused glow, and the street still sees the full picture.
  • With heavy drapes. Use the inner track of a double rod or a separate rod so the drapes still close over the lights. It's a cozy combination for a bedroom; see /curtain-lights-for-bedroom/.

Keep the USB controller and charger where air can circulate, not wedged against a radiator or buried in fabric.

Condensation, cold glass and wet sills

Most condensation in a home forms when warm, moist air meets a cool surface, and it's especially noticeable in winter when heating meets cold glass, above all single glazing. The result is beads of water on the pane and puddles on the sill, often first thing in the morning.

  • Don't let the strands rest against wet glass; a small gap helps.
  • Keep the USB controller, plug and charger off the sill, where water collects. Lumalyn says the USB plug must stay dry, and a phone charger isn't built for damp sills at all.
  • If condensation is heavy, better ventilation or a dehumidifier helps the room as well as the lights.

Frequently asked questions

Should curtain lights go inside or outside the window?

Inside, for most homes. The glass protects the lights from weather and they're still visible from the street. Outside only works in a sheltered spot unless the set is rated for rain; Lumalyn's IP44 curtain is for indoor and sheltered outdoor use, not open rain.

How do I stop window lights reflecting in the glass?

Hang the curtain close to the pane so its reflection lines up with the real LEDs, switch off bright room lights, and lower the curtain's brightness when you're watching it from inside.

Can I leave curtain lights in the window all year?

Yes, indoors. Check the cable and plug now and then, keep them off wet sills, and use a timer so the lights only run when you want them. A pixel curtain can change scenes with the seasons.

Will condensation damage window curtain lights?

Keep the strands off wet glass and the USB plug and charger off the sill. Lumalyn's curtain is splash resistant (IP44), but Lumalyn says the USB plug must stay dry.

Can I use curtain lights with blinds?

Yes. Mount the lights on a tension rod or hooks between the glass and the blinds. Lowered blinds then act as a clean backdrop from the street and block the glow at night.
